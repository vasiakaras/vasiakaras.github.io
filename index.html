About

I am supervised by Dr. Amori Yee Mikami. My dissertation looks at how people search for information about ADHD online, how that content shapes what they believe and do, and how clinicians can respond to it.

As a therapist, I work in a collaborative, warm, and evidence-based way. I combine cognitive-behavioural techniques with compassion-focused strategies so that treatment fits each person. I have trained in CBT, DBT, and psychodynamic therapy. I have worked with individuals, couples, and groups on concerns including anxiety, depression, OCD, trauma, and relationship and sexual health.

I grew up in Greece, and that gives me a multicultural lens: a strong sense of how cultural context shapes the way we understand ourselves, our relationships, and our sexuality. I try to centre the values, identities, and lived experiences each person brings. Before UBC, I earned an M.A. in Psychology (Forensic) and undergraduate degrees in Psychology and in Neuroscience & Mental Health at Carleton University.

Research

My research centres on digital citizenship: how people interact with the online world and how that interaction affects their lives. It follows three streams.

Mental health information online

How people find and judge information about ADHD and other mental health conditions on platforms like TikTok, how that content influences self-perception and treatment attitudes, and how to use social media in ways that support wellbeing.

Online sexual communication & consent

How consent is communicated, assumed, or ignored in sexting and other digitally mediated sexual encounters, and the gender norms and attitudes behind these patterns.

Technology-facilitated sexual violence

Non-consensual intimate image dissemination, cyberflashing, and AI-generated (deepfake) sexual content. This work covers who perpetrates these acts and why, how survivors are affected and cope, and where the law falls short.
